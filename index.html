The proof that falls every morning. Performed, kept, and ready to hand over.

A Canadian payment firm has to know, at the end of every day, how much of the money it holds belongs to each of its customers, and it has to produce that evidence whenever it is asked for. 4orm keeps that record, checks it against the account the money sits in, and builds the examiner's pack on demand. We never touch the money.

The work today

None of this is new work. All of it is being done by hand.

At almost every firm that carries the duty, each of these five jobs falls to one person who has another job as well.

Screen What it answers What it replaces today
Today Whether anything is late A wall calendar, a reminder in somebody's phone, and hoping.
Money Whether yesterday balanced A spreadsheet with a tab for each day, built by somebody who has since left.
Proof Where the paper is Eleven folders on a shared drive and an email trail.
Filings Whether the report is ready Three weeks in March rebuilding the year from memory.
Exam pack What an examiner receives A person with other work assembling a box for an examiner.

A record made on the morning it happened is worth something to an examiner. A record built in March out of memory is not.

Before you read on

What this site shows, and what it deliberately does not.

We would rather say this plainly on the way in than have you wonder about it on the way out.

What is on this site

  • The five screens, and the question each one answers.
  • An illustrative dashboard, drawn rather than photographed, with every figure in it invented for the picture.
  • The public law: what it requires, when it started, and what breaking it costs.
  • The things we refuse to build, stated before anyone has to ask.

What is not on this site

  • How any of it works. Patent applications are pending and the design is confidential.
  • A real customer. No balance, name or timestamp on this site belongs to anybody.
  • A live environment. Nothing on this site is connected to anything. No system, account or record shown here is real.
  • Advice. What the statutes require of your firm is a question for your own counsel.
