What happens, in order

The record is written while the work is being done.

Not assembled afterwards from an inbox, a drive and somebody who has since left. These four steps happen in this order, on every file, without anyone stopping to do paperwork.

01

Captured

What the customer wanted, what was known that day, which options were considered, what was recommended and the reason for it.

02

Joined

Each fact is joined to its source, the decision to the facts it rested on, and any later change to the decision it changed.

03

Surfaced

An incomplete file appears on the board the day it happens, across everyone acting in the name of the firm.

04

Produced

When a reviewer, a regulator or a complaint asks, the file is produced on request rather than reconstructed.

One deal, from first call to complaint

The evidence was being created the whole time.

Nobody set out to build a file. This is one renewal, and the record it left behind without anyone doing extra work.

Day 1

The first conversation

The borrower says the payment matters more than the rate, and that they may move within three years. Both are captured as stated, on the day they were said.

Day 1

What was known then

Income, the existing term, the penalty position and the rate environment on that date. Later this is what separates a reasonable recommendation from one that only looks wrong in hindsight.

Day 3

Three options, two declined

The alternatives that were considered, and the reason each was set aside. This is the part that is almost never written down anywhere the firm owns.

Day 4

The recommendation, with its reason

A five year fixed, chosen for the payment certainty the borrower asked for, with the prepayment terms disclosed and acknowledged.

Day 26

Something changes

The borrower changes employer. The recommendation is re-checked against the new circumstances, and the check is dated against what was known before, so the two are never confused.

Month 19

A complaint arrives

The borrower says they were never told about the penalty. The file already holds the disclosure, the acknowledgement, the date and the person. Nobody spends a week rebuilding a year from memory.

Where the line sits

We do not replace the relationship, and we never take the judgement.

These are refusals rather than a roadmap. They are the questions a compliance officer asks in the second meeting, so we would rather answer them in the first.

It never decides

The platform does not determine whether a recommendation is suitable. It creates the context and the evidence that let the customer, the professional and the firm demonstrate why it was. The professional keeps the recommendation.

It never holds money

No client funds pass through it and none are ever held. The platform sits beside the money, never in front of it, and it produces the record rather than the transaction.

It never performs your review

4orm does not perform, conduct or sign an independent review. It produces the material the review runs on. The reviewer stays independent and the firm keeps the duty and the signature.

A missing record is not proof that anybody did anything wrong, and this platform never says it is. A recommendation can be right and still be impossible to demonstrate. That is a record failure, and it is the one thing here that gets solved.
