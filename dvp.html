Delivery versus payment

Two legs. One custody confirmation. One write. One finality.

Traditional settlement decouples the cash and asset legs by days. Delivery versus payment binds them: both legs and the custody record commit in the same write to the canonical ledger, or none does. No reconciliation. No counterparty risk window.

The mechanic, in three steps.

1. Match

The order book matches a bid and an ask. Eligibility for both counterparties was verified pre-trade. The matcher emits a settlement instruction containing the asset leg, the cash leg, and the custody confirmation.

2. Commit

The settlement instruction is one transaction against the canonical ledger. The asset transfer, the cash transfer, and the custody record write together. There is no intermediate state.

3. Finalize

On commit, the trade is final. The supervisory feed sees the commit. The member firms see the commit. The regulator sees the commit. One record. One time.

The value of DvP.

DvP is a property of the settlement layer, not a workflow. When the asset leg, the cash leg, and the supervisory record commit in the same write to the canonical ledger, four things become impossible.

No counterparty risk window.

There is no moment where one party has paid and the other has not yet delivered. Both legs commit as one write, or neither commits at all.

No half-settled state.

Either all three legs (asset, cash, custody) finalize together, or nothing changes. There is no intermediate condition to reason about, unwind, or report separately.

No reconciliation between systems.

There is one canonical record. There is nothing to reconcile between a trading book, a settlement book, and a custody book. Regulators, member firms, and investors all read from the same source.

No failed-trade workflow at the layer level.

A failed commit is a rolled-back transaction, not a stuck trade waiting on a manual rescue. The workflow that exists in legacy settlement because trades fail simply has nothing to grip on to.

This is the design choice that makes settlement finality a property of the layer, not a property of speed. A fast settlement is not the same as a final settlement. DvP at commit gives you both.
